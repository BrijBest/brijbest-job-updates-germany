Do you qualify for the
Chancenkarte?

Germany's Opportunity Card lets you live in Germany up to 12 months while job hunting — no job offer needed. Answer 8 questions and get your eligibility score right now.

Step 1 — Prerequisite (Required)
Question 1 of 8 · Mandatory Prerequisite
What is your highest completed qualification?
The Chancenkarte requires either a university degree or a vocational qualification of at least 2 years. This is non-negotiable — points only count if you clear this baseline first.
Prerequisite not met. The Chancenkarte requires at least a recognised vocational qualification or university degree. Without one you cannot apply. Consider a Job Seeker Visa or EU Blue Card as alternatives.
Step 2 — Points (You Need 6 or More)
Question 2 of 8 · Language Skills · Up to 2 pts
What is your German language level?
German proficiency is the highest-weighted single criterion. Even A1 earns a point — and B2+ can be decisive for reaching 6.
Question 3 of 8 · Language Skills · Up to 1 pt
What is your English language level?
English points stack on top of German points — not instead of. Strong English and any German = up to 3 language points combined.
Question 4 of 8 · Experience · Up to 2 pts
How many years of professional experience do you have in your field?
Experience in your field of qualification counts — not general work history. Country of experience does not matter.
Question 5 of 8 · Shortage Occupation · Up to 1 pt
Is your profession on Germany's shortage occupation list (Engpassberufe)?
🏥 Nurses / Pfleger💊 Doctors (specialist) 💻 Software Engineers📊 Data Scientists ⚙️ Mechanical Engineers⚡ Electricians 🔧 Skilled Tradespeople🏗️ Civil Engineers 👶 Childcare Workers🚛 HGV Drivers 🔐 Cybersecurity🤖 Automation Engineers
If unsure, check the BA's Engpassanalyse. Many professions qualify that people don't realise.
Question 6 of 8 · Age · Up to 2 pts
What is your current age?
Age is one of the two highest-weighted criteria alongside German language. Germany prioritises long-term labour market integration.
Question 7 of 8 · Germany Connection · Up to 1 pt
Have you legally lived in Germany for at least 6 months in the last 5 years?
Includes time on student visa, previous work visa, language course, or any other legal residence — shows prior integration.
Question 8 of 8 · Family Ties · Up to 1 pt
Do you have a spouse, parent, or child who is a German citizen or has permanent residence in Germany?
Close family means spouse/registered partner, parent, or child. This demonstrates a personal tie to Germany beyond professional motivation.
